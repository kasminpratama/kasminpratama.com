CCTV & Surveillance Systems.

I work with CCTV systems from the physical installation through to configuration, recording, network integration, and final system testing.

OVERVIEW

A surveillance system is more than a camera.

A CCTV installation only becomes useful when the complete system works as intended: cameras capture the required areas, the recorder receives the streams, storage retains the footage for the required period, and the network allows the relevant devices to communicate reliably.

My work is focused on the practical side of that process. I handle installation, device configuration, recording setup, network integration, troubleshooting, and functional testing rather than treating the camera as an isolated product.

HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS

From image capture to usable recording.

01

Camera

The camera captures the scene and, in an IP-based system, sends the video stream across the network. Camera selection and placement should follow the area that actually needs to be observed.

02

Recorder

A DVR or NVR receives and manages surveillance recordings. The recorder must be matched to the camera type, channel requirements, recording method, and storage design.

03

Storage

Recording capacity is a system-design question, not simply a hard-disk capacity question. Camera count, resolution, frame rate, bitrate, codec, recording schedule, and retention period all affect the storage requirement.

04

Network

IP cameras and network recorders depend on reliable connectivity. Addressing, switching, routing, and access paths need to be considered when surveillance equipment is integrated into an existing network.

DVR, NVR & IP SYSTEMS

The recorder has to match the system architecture.

System Typical architecture What needs attention
DVR Camera signals are brought to the recorder through the appropriate physical connections. Camera compatibility, cabling, power, recorder channels, recording settings, and storage.
NVR Network cameras communicate with the recorder over an IP network. IP addressing, network connectivity, camera compatibility, recorder capacity, recording settings, and storage.
IP surveillance environment Cameras, recorders, switches, routers, and client devices communicate through network infrastructure. Network design, addressing, segmentation where required, bandwidth, access, and fault isolation.
The correct architecture depends on the equipment and the installation requirement. A recorder should not be selected only by channel count; camera compatibility, recording capability, storage, network requirements, and the intended operating environment also matter.

IP INTEROPERABILITY

Compatibility should be checked, not assumed.

In an IP surveillance system, equipment from different manufacturers may be considered for integration. ONVIF profiles provide a defined way to identify supported functions between conformant devices and clients. Profile T covers advanced video streaming, while Profile G addresses recording and retrieval functions.

In practice, I treat interoperability as something to verify against the actual camera and recorder models. The presence of an "ONVIF" label alone is not enough to conclude that every function will work across every combination of equipment.

Technical reference: ONVIF Profiles, Profile T, and Profile G.

STORAGE & RECORDING

Retention starts with the recording requirement.

There is no single hard-disk capacity that is correct for every CCTV system. The storage requirement changes with the number of cameras and with how each camera records.

Resolution, frame rate, bitrate, codec, continuous or event-based recording, and the number of days of retention all affect the result. Storage should therefore be calculated from the intended recording configuration rather than estimated from camera count alone.

01

Camera count

More active streams generally mean more data to record and retain.

02

Bitrate

The configured video bitrate has a direct effect on the amount of recorded data.

03

Recording mode

Continuous and event-based recording produce different storage requirements.

04

Retention

The required number of days determines how long the recorded data must be kept.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Questions that matter before an installation.

Is CCTV installation only about choosing the right camera?

No. The camera is one part of the system. Installation also involves the recorder, storage, power, cabling, network connectivity where applicable, configuration, and testing.

What is the difference between a DVR and an NVR?

The main difference is the system architecture and how video reaches the recorder. DVR systems are commonly used with cameras connected through their corresponding physical video interfaces, while NVR systems are designed for network cameras communicating over IP networks.

How much hard-disk capacity does a CCTV system need?

It depends on the recording design. Camera count, bitrate, resolution, frame rate, codec, recording mode, and retention period all affect the required capacity. A capacity estimate without those parameters is not a reliable system calculation.

Can cameras and recorders from different brands work together?

They can in some configurations, but compatibility must be checked against the actual models and supported interfaces or profiles. ONVIF provides standardized profiles for interoperability, but it does not mean every feature of every manufacturer's product will be interchangeable.

What should be tested before a CCTV system is handed over?

At minimum, the relevant installation should be checked for camera operation, device communication, live view, recording, playback, and the required network access. The exact test scope depends on the system architecture.
